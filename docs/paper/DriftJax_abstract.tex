\begin{abstract}
DriftJax is a one-dimensional drift--diffusion--Poisson solver for
photovoltaic device simulation and design sensitivity analysis, implemented
in JAX with implicit-function-theorem gradients of the converged discrete
system, numerically verified against finite differences on selected
configurations. The forward
solver combines Scharfetter--Gummel fluxes, an analytically assembled
block-tridiagonal Jacobian, and a direct pivoted banded elimination
(LAPACK \code{dgbsv}) that requires $O(N\cdot\mathrm{kl}\cdot
\mathrm{ku})$ work per Newton step for $N$ mesh nodes. Reverse-mode
sensitivities use pivoted dense-LU adjoint factorisation with
$O(N^3)$ backward cost; row-equilibration diagnosis identifies the
 conditioning trade-off between structured and dense paths.
Verification covers analytical--automatic-differentiation Jacobian
agreement ($5\times10^{-17}$ relative), dark-equilibrium mass-action
law consistency, current conservation ($<10^{-17}$), mesh convergence
across five refinement levels, and directional Taylor tests with
$O(h)$ scaling. Controlled single-architecture benchmarks quantify
the forward and adjoint cost on a modern CPU. Example applications
demonstrate derivative-based sensitivity ranking, constrained
band-gap/thickness optimisation, and synthetic current--voltage
curve recovery.
\end{abstract}
